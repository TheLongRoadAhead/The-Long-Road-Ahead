Chapter 1

Escape from Miami

← Return to Homepage

The humid Miami air was thick with the scent of saltwater and decay. For two straight years, the rooftop of the abandoned high-rise had been our fortress. It was a solid setup—high above the street-level flesh-eaters, with rain-catchers that kept us alive since the virus collapsed the world back in 2024.

Caleb sat hunched over a makeshift radio deck rigged to a dying car battery. At seventeen, his face was already that of a hardened survivor, though his posture remained completely nonchalant. Next to him, thirteen-year-old Mikey was casually tossing a rusted pocket knife into a wooden crate, cracking jokes to break the heavy silence.

"If we stay here any longer, bru, I’m going to start growing gills," Mikey muttered, wiping sweat from his forehead. "Even the zombies downstairs look too tired to hunt in this heat."

Caleb didn't blink. He adjusted a copper wire on the receiver. "Just keep an eye on the stairwell door, Mike. The tripwires won’t hold forever if a herd pushes through from the lobby."

Suddenly, the radio speaker hissed violently. A wave of harsh static cut through the room, making both brothers freeze. Mikey dropped his knife. Caleb leaned in closer, his casual demeanor instantly vanishing as a heavily distorted, metallic voice broke through the white noise.

"...if anyone... receiving this... this is Commander Heyes... repeating... we have established a fortified refugee base... Los Angeles, California... survivors can find shelter at the local... camp... repeat... L.A. is safe..."

The signal violently snapped, returning to dead static.

Mikey stared at the speaker, his mouth open. "Caleb... was that... Dad?"

Before Caleb could answer, a loud, metallic *snap* echoed from the lower floors, followed by the unmistakable sound of breaking wood. The stairwell tripwires had just been triggered. Down below, a chorus of guttural, frantic screeches began to rise up the concrete shafts. A massive herd had breached the ground floor.

Caleb didn't panic. He stood up, grabbed his heavy machete, and slung his pre-packed bug-out bag over his shoulder. The nonchalant mask slid right back onto his face.

"That was him," Caleb said calmly, checking the magazine of his pistol. "Grab your kit, Mikey. We’ve got a four-thousand-kilometer walk ahead of us, and our ride just got canceled."

Mikey grabbed his pack with a grim smile. "Right behind you, bro."
